Analytics Engineering & Governance: From Fragmented Spreadsheets to a Governed Semantic Layer

The Demonstration: I audited and rebuilt a fragmented data environment, standardizing metric definitions across Finance, Sales, and Marketing, and delivered a fully documented executive reporting suite with a structured handoff. (Note: This lab uses synthetic retail data designed to mirror real-world enterprise engagements.)

The Situation

The business was experiencing rapid YoY revenue growth, but leadership lacked visibility into regional profitability. The issue was not a lack of tooling—they had SQL Server, modern CRMs, and ad platforms. The issue was that company-wide metrics were being manually built by operations managers in isolated Excel spreadsheets instead of a centralized data warehouse.

Because the analytics environment lacked a governed definition layer, the Finance and Marketing teams were consistently arriving at completely different numbers for the exact same metrics. They needed a seasoned Analytics Engineer to intervene, migrate the manual reports, create interoperable tables, and establish a single source of truth.

Why this engagement mattered

Inflexible reporting is a common byproduct of databases and dashboards that grow without centralized ownership. When calculations accumulate across ad-hoc queries without a unifying standard, the problem is rarely the data itself—it is the absence of a canonical definition layer.

Resolving this requires more than just writing SQL. It requires stakeholder alignment on what metrics actually mean, disciplined upstream implementation in the ETL pipeline, and creating a concrete artifact that teams can align around. My approach prioritizes definition alignment before model development, ensuring that what gets built reflects what the business actually agreed on.

My Approach

I worked across three distinct workstreams to stabilize and automate the environment:

  • Metric Standardization & Governance: Rebuilt the transformation logic. Calculations previously distributed across individual analysts’ spreadsheets were moved upstream into SQL Server views, versioned, and documented.
  • Semantic Layer Deployment: Engineered a governed Power BI Semantic Model using DAX. This handled the complex time-intelligence and currency formatting centrally, ensuring metric definitions were not replicated at the dashboard layer.
  • Executive Dashboard Buildout: Developed a highly interactive, 3-page reporting suite covering macro trends, root-cause anomaly detection (Decomposition Trees), and What-If scenario modeling.

The Deployed Asset

The Outcome

After this rollout, metric definitions were unified in a single governed Power BI Semantic Model, with Finance, Sales, and Marketing pulling from the exact same source. At handoff, the internal team took full ownership of a maintainable, automated codebase.

Architectural Decision Record (ADR)

Strategic Rationale: Power BI Semantic Models vs. SSAS Tabular

When designing the semantic layer for this rollout, I actively chose to bypass traditional SQL Server Analysis Services (SSAS) in favor of a cloud-native Power BI Semantic Model.

Under the hood, both architectures rely on the exact same VertiPaq engine. However, Microsoft's strategic focus has shifted heavily toward Power BI and Microsoft Fabric. By bypassing on-premises SSAS, this architecture supports immediate Git integration, dynamic Calculation Groups, Entra ID Row-Level Security, and is immediately scalable for future DirectLake adoptions.
